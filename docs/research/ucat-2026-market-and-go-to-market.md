# UCAT ANZ 2026 market and go-to-market evidence

Date: 19 August 2026  
Scope: market size, geography, annual demand cycle, official-preparation constraints, and a primary-source competitor snapshot for Altitutor UCAT Unlimited  
Method: official UCAT/UCAT ANZ pages and current first-party vendor product/pricing pages. Competitor claims are reported as vendor claims, not independently verified performance evidence. Calculations and go-to-market implications are labelled as such.

## Executive answer

**One thousand paid users by 31 December 2026 is not a useful base-case launch target.** It is mathematically possible, but it would equal **5.9% of the entire 2025 UCAT ANZ candidate cohort** (`1,000 / 16,950`), and the 2026 preparation season has already ended: testing ran from 1 July to 5 August 2026. At 19 August, most immediate prep demand has therefore expired; the remaining 2026 acquisition window is mainly the early edge of the 2027 cohort rather than another full cohort ([2025 official summary statistics](https://www.ucat.edu.au/media/1581/summary-statistics-for-2025.pdf), [2026 dates](https://www.ucat.edu.au/about-ucat-anz/ucat-anz-test-cycle/)).

Treat **1,000 cumulative paid students across the full 2027 test cycle** as an ambitious market-share objective, not the initial forecast. For the December 2026 launch period, set a learning target after establishing the current funnel baseline. A defensible provisional ladder is:

| Target through 31 December 2026 | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| 50 first-time paid customers | minimum proof that acquisition, activation, checkout and delivered value work beyond the founder's immediate network |
| 100 first-time paid customers | strong early traction for an off-season launch and enough customers for qualitative cohort analysis |
| 250 first-time paid customers | stretch outcome requiring repeatable channels, not only founder-led outreach |
| 1,000 first-time paid customers | exceptional off-season outcome; do not use as the operating forecast without funnel evidence |

These thresholds are **planning judgement**, not market benchmarks. Replace them after 4–6 weeks with a bottom-up target based on qualified visitors, free-account activation, free-to-paid conversion, retention and channel capacity. Do not use “paid users” without specifying whether it means first purchase, currently active subscriptions, or retained subscribers: seasonal cancellation after a candidate sits the test makes those three measures very different.

## Market size and trend

The official UCAT ANZ overview describes the market as “around 15,000” candidates per year. The most recent final figure available is **16,950 candidates in the main 2025 testing window**, excluding a small number who sat outside that window ([UCAT ANZ overview](https://www.ucat.edu.au/about-ucat-anz/), [2025 official summary statistics](https://www.ucat.edu.au/media/1581/summary-statistics-for-2025.pdf)).

Official historical statistics list 15,255 candidates in 2024, up from 14,176 in 2023 and about 13,900–14,100 in 2020–2022. The increase from 15,255 in 2024 to 16,950 in 2025 was approximately **11.1%** (calculation), but one year's growth should not be extrapolated as a durable rate ([official test statistics](https://www.ucat.edu.au/results/test-statistics-2025/), [2025 summary](https://www.ucat.edu.au/media/1581/summary-statistics-for-2025.pdf)).

The 2026 total is not yet available. UCAT ANZ says preliminary statistics will be published in late August and summary statistics/percentiles in September ([2026 test statistics](https://www.ucat.edu.au/results/test-statistics/)). Update this note and any market-share denominator when that figure is released.

### What a 1,000-customer goal implies

Using 16,950 candidates as the latest known denominator:

| Cumulative paid customers in one complete cohort | Share of latest annual candidate pool |
| ---: | ---: |
| 170 | 1.0% |
| 339 | 2.0% |
| 509 | 3.0% |
| 1,000 | 5.9% |

This is a ceiling-style denominator, not a count of buyers of commercial preparation. It includes candidates who will use only free official material, choose a competitor, buy multiple providers, or buy no preparation. Conversely, some eligible early preparers may buy before their test year, and repeat candidates create revenue opportunities in more than one annual cycle.

## Audience and geography

UCAT ANZ is available to people undertaking or having completed their final year of secondary schooling, or higher. In practical school-year terms, Australian Year 12 and New Zealand Year 13 candidates can sit; Australian Year 11 and New Zealand Year 12 candidates cannot yet sit, while university students may be eligible ([official eligibility rules](https://www.ucat.edu.au/about-ucat-anz/eligibility/)).

The official consortium list contains **18 institutions: 16 in Australia and two in New Zealand**. Courses include medicine, dentistry/oral health and clinical-science or provisional-entry pathways. Auckland and Otago are the two New Zealand institutions ([official university list](https://www.ucat.edu.au/about-ucat-anz/universities/)). Test centres cover every Australian state and territory, six named New Zealand cities, and selected overseas Pearson locations ([official test-centre locations](https://www.ucat.edu.au/register/test-centre-locations/)).

This supports a primary Australia/New Zealand positioning with geographic localisation around university and school communities. It does **not** establish state-level candidate counts; no current official state split was found, so ad budgets should not be allocated by presumed market size without first-party Altitutor data or a credible admissions dataset.

UCAT ANZ 2026 results can also be submitted to participating UK universities for 2027 entry. A candidate cannot sit both UCAT ANZ and UCAT UK in the same year, and the 2026 UK results-collection form closes on 22 October 2026 ([official results guidance](https://www.ucat.edu.au/results/ucat-anz-results/)). This is a small adjacent message for internationally oriented ANZ candidates, not evidence for a separate broad international launch.

## Annual demand cycle

The official 2026 milestones were ([official 2026 test cycle](https://www.ucat.edu.au/about-ucat-anz/ucat-anz-test-cycle/)):

| Milestone | 2026 date | Commercial implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Concession and access-arrangement applications open | 16 February | official attention begins; preparation intent should already be building |
| Booking opens | 3 March | high-intent acquisition trigger |
| Normal booking closes | 15 May | urgency increases; late booking still possible |
| Final late booking closes | 5 June | cohort is substantially fixed |
| Testing window | 1 July–5 August | peak preparation, mock and review demand; each student's demand ends on their individual test date |
| Results delivered to universities | early September | preparation demand has ended; admissions/interview needs take over |

Results are valid only for that admissions cycle: UCAT ANZ 2026 is used for 2027 entry and cannot be carried into a later year ([official university guidance](https://www.ucat.edu.au/about-ucat-anz/universities/)). The product therefore has structurally high seasonality and a natural churn event when the student sits the exam.

### Applied launch timing

The acquisition year should be planned as a cohort cycle rather than a calendar-year subscription business:

1. **August–November:** earn trust with the next cohort, recruit founding users and school/community partners, collect qualitative evidence, and instrument the funnel. Paid volume is likely to be modest.
2. **December–February:** publish preparation-start resources, referrals and early-cohort onboarding; validate channel economics before the main rush.
3. **March–May:** scale the channels that already activate students; booking deadlines create high intent without needing manufactured urgency.
4. **June–early August:** conversion and usage peak. Market around genuine product value—timed practice, realistic mocks, targeted review and progress—not fear.
5. **After each student's test:** stop prep prompts, collect feedback and referrals appropriately, and measure realised retention by study stage rather than trying to prevent rational end-of-cycle churn.

Only 2026 dates are official today. Future cycle dates should not be represented as fixed until UCAT ANZ publishes them.

## Product context created by the official free alternative

The current test is computer-based, just under two hours, and contains four separately timed subtests: Verbal Reasoning (44 questions, 22 minutes), Decision Making (35, 37 minutes), Quantitative Reasoning (36, 26 minutes), and Situational Judgement (69, 26 minutes) ([official test format](https://www.ucat.edu.au/about-ucat-anz/test-format/)). Abstract Reasoning is no longer part of the current test format.

UCAT ANZ explicitly directs candidates to its free official materials and does not endorse commercial providers. It warns that commercial questions, screen formats and mock scoring can be unrepresentative or misleading ([official preparation warning](https://www.ucat.edu.au/prepare/)). Altitutor should therefore avoid any implication that it is official, endorsed, or a guaranteed score predictor.

The official sequence is preparation plan → tour and question tutorials → question banks → four timed practice tests ([official preparation resources](https://www.ucat.edu.au/prepare/preparation-resources/)). The official site also identifies concrete experience gaps: question-bank progress is not retained; practice-test results are not saved and no score is provided; and the materials are intended for desktop ([official practice tests and question banks](https://www.ucat.edu.au/prepare/practice-tests/)).

These facts create a credible complementary position for Altitutor:

- retain work and show progress over time;
- help a student decide what to practise next;
- provide targeted review and explanations;
- support study planning and accountability;
- reproduce the relevant interface, tools and pacing carefully;
- use mobile for planning, review and short practice while being honest that the live test is desktop-based;
- encourage final calibration with the official representative materials.

The defensible promise is not “better or more official questions.” It is **a more usable preparation system around ongoing practice, feedback and decisions**, with the official material retained as the final source of format truth.

| NIE | current catalogue includes A$195 workshop, A$275 UCAT Advanced Course (Medify and workshop), A$729 UCAT/interview comprehensive course, and A$1,390 12-hour tuition | offers live workshops, bundled third-party platform access, masterclasses and tutoring | The catalogue demonstrates a broad assisted-service market from low hundreds to four figures; it does not establish sales volume or outcomes ([official catalogue](https://www.nie.edu.au/products-listing-menu/ucat-r-preparation-courses/ucat-course-packages-workshops)) |

The primary-source evidence supports three broad alternatives in the market:

1. free official preparation;
2. self-serve question-bank/mock platforms at recurring or season-access prices;
3. workshops, classes and tutoring from hundreds to thousands of dollars.

Altitutor should not attempt to win on raw question count alone: both major self-serve competitors advertise 20,000+ questions. A lower entry price may help, but the more sustainable wedge is a distinct learning loop, founder responsiveness, clear free value and evidence that students can decide and act more effectively inside the product.

## Integrity and marketing constraints

UCAT's website terms restrict its materials to personal, non-commercial use and prohibit commercial reuse without permission; linking is permitted only when fair and lawful and when it does not imply association or endorsement. UCAT trademarks and logos require permission ([UCAT ANZ website terms](https://www.ucat.edu.au/website-terms-of-use/)).

Candidate misconduct rules also prohibit using live-test questions, content or information for purposes other than sitting the test, and people affiliated with a UCAT coaching business are not eligible to sit ([official candidate-misconduct rules](https://www.ucat.edu.au/sit/candidate-misconduct/), [official eligibility rules](https://www.ucat.edu.au/about-ucat-anz/eligibility/)). Operationally:

- never solicit or ingest recalled live-test content;
- screen question authors and tutors for how source material was obtained;
- do not copy official questions, graphics or test assets into a commercial product without permission;
- do not use “official”, the UCAT logo, or copy that implies affiliation;
- link to official resources and position them as the final representative reference.

## Measurements needed before committing to the 1,000 target

The next target should be built from a funnel, not a top-down aspiration. At minimum, measure weekly and by acquisition source:

`qualified landing-page visitor → account created → first meaningful practice → second active day → limit/paywall viewed → checkout started → first payment → active practice in week 2 → retained until intended test stage`

Record test year and intended test date early. Without them, low December retention can be confused with product failure, and rational post-test churn can be confused with preventable churn.

For each organic tactic—Reddit, Facebook groups, founder conversations, school/community outreach, cards and referrals—use a distinct source/referral code or tagged landing link. Report customer count, activation, paid conversion, cost/time per activated paid student, refund/cancellation and retained practice. Do not optimise for comments, clicks or sign-ups alone.

Once a baseline exists, a target can be calculated as:

`qualified reach × visit rate × signup rate × activation rate × paid conversion rate = first-time paid customers`

Then constrain it by realistic founder capacity and channel saturation. If the December model cannot reach the desired number under plausible rates, change the date, scope (for example, full 2027 cohort), or resourcing—not the measurement definition.

## Immediate decisions supported by this research

1. Define the 2026 launch goal as first-time paid customers plus activated free students and learning milestones, not year-end active subscriptions alone.
2. Use 50 / 100 / 250 paid customers as provisional December floor/base/stretch learning targets; reserve 1,000 for the full 2027-cycle ambition until the funnel proves otherwise.
3. Build acquisition before the official February–March attention spike; do not spend heavily in the current post-test trough merely to chase a calendar deadline.
4. Position Unlimited as the ongoing feedback, planning and progress layer that complements official materials.
5. Avoid raw question-count warfare and unqualified score-prediction claims.
6. Instrument test date, acquisition source, activation, paid conversion and stage-aware retention before scaling ads or sponsorships.
7. Recheck 2026 candidate statistics when UCAT publishes them in late August/September, and recheck competitor pricing before any public comparison.
