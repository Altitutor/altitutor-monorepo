# UCAT SJT Most/Least scoring

Date: 2026-08-15

## Question

For Situational Judgement questions that ask candidates to choose one most
appropriate and one least appropriate action from three actions:

- does the instruction that both actions must be selected mean both must also be
  correct before any marks are awarded;
- is partial credit available when the completed response is not fully correct;
  and
- is Altitutor's per-destination `4/2/0` scoring a defensible internal model?

## Conclusion

The best-supported answer is **no, the item is not all-or-nothing**. Selecting
both actions is a response-completion requirement. It is not evidence that both
selections must be correct before any marks are awarded.

The current UCAT ANZ scoring page says that SJT questions receive full marks
when the response matches the correct answer and partial marks when the response
is close to the correct answer. The current UK page publishes the same rule.
([UCAT ANZ scoring](https://www.ucat.edu.au/about-ucat-anz/scoring/),
[UCAT UK test format and scoring](https://www.ucat.ac.uk/about-ucat/test-format-and-scoring/))

Pearson's official technical reports provide format-specific evidence. The 2022
report classifies a Most/Least response containing only one placement as
**incomplete**. It does not say that a response with two placements but only one
correct endpoint receives zero. The report also describes SJT scoring as
polytomous and its Most/Least statistics imply a maximum raw score of eight.
([2022 UCAT technical report, pp. 48, 52–53](https://www.ucat.ac.uk/media/1546/ucat-2022-technical-report.pdf))

The public official sources do **not** publish the exact score awarded to each of
the six possible completed Most/Least permutations. Consequently, Altitutor's
current per-destination `4/2/0` rule, summed to a maximum of eight, is consistent
with the public evidence but is not proven by it. It should remain labelled an
internal/provisional scoring policy rather than an official UCAT rule.

## Completion is not correctness

The attached question says that no marks are received unless both the most and
least appropriate actions are selected. Grammatically and operationally, that
sets a condition on whether the response is complete; it does not say “unless
both selections are correct”.

The official 2022 technical report confirms that interpretation in its candidate
removal criteria. It labels Most/Least items with “Only one response” as
“incomplete”. This is direct evidence that the two placements form one combined
response and that one placement alone is not a completed attempt.
([2022 UCAT technical report, p. 48](https://www.ucat.ac.uk/media/1546/ucat-2022-technical-report.pdf))

Once both placements have been made, the general official SJT scoring rule
applies: exact responses receive full marks and responses close to the correct
answer receive partial marks. Nothing in the reviewed official material creates
an all-or-nothing exception for Most/Least questions.
([UCAT ANZ scoring](https://www.ucat.edu.au/about-ucat-anz/scoring/),
[UCAT UK test format and scoring](https://www.ucat.ac.uk/about-ucat/test-format-and-scoring/))

## What the technical statistics establish

The technical reports define item facility as the mean score expressed as a
proportion or percentage of the maximum score. This allows the Most/Least
maximum to be recovered from the published values:

| Report | Most/Least mean | Facility | Implied maximum |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2022 | 6.79 | 85% | about 8 (`6.79 / 0.85 = 7.99`) |
| 2025 | 7.20 | 0.90 | 8 (`7.20 / 0.90 = 8`) |

The 2022 report additionally says the SJT makes extensive use of polytomous
scoring. Together with the candidate-facing partial-credit rule, these data are
inconsistent with treating the screenshot's completion warning as proof of an
official “both correct or zero” policy.
([2022 UCAT technical report, pp. 48, 52–53](https://www.ucat.ac.uk/media/1546/ucat-2022-technical-report.pdf),
[2025 UCAT technical report, pp. 59, 62–63](https://www.ucat.ac.uk/media/1726/ucat-2025-technical-report-final.pdf))

These statistics establish the eight-point maximum, but they do not reveal all
attainable discrete scores or map each possible placement pair to a score.

## Assessment of Altitutor's `4/2/0` policy

Altitutor currently scores each destination independently:

- `4` for placing the exact endpoint action in that destination;
- `2` for placing the unkeyed middle action there; and
- `0` for placing the opposite endpoint action there.

The two destination scores are added, producing a maximum of eight. Because an
action cannot be used in both destinations, the six completed responses produce
combined totals of `8`, `6`, `6`, `2`, `2`, or `0`; a completed response cannot
produce `4` under this model.

This model is defensible as an internal approximation because it:

- enforces the official completion requirement;
- permits partial credit as the official candidate guidance requires;
- preserves ordinal closeness between endpoint, middle and opposite choices; and
- matches the eight-point maximum implied by multiple official technical reports.

However, no reviewed public UCAT or UCAT ANZ source confirms that the middle
action is worth exactly two points per destination, that endpoints are scored
independently, or that the six permutations map to `8/6/6/2/2/0`. Those details
must not be represented to learners as official.

A whole-question rule such as “4 if both are correct, 2 if one is correct, 0 if
neither is correct” would also be an internal approximation, but it is less
closely aligned with the published eight-point technical metric and discards the
difference between selecting the middle action and reversing the endpoints.
There is no official evidence supporting a change to all-or-nothing scoring.

## Product recommendation

Keep the current scoring boundary provisional and clearly documented. Do not
change Most/Least questions to “both correct or zero” based on the attached
instruction. If the UI shows marks, explain that they are Altitutor practice
marks approximating UCAT partial-credit behaviour, not the proprietary formula
used to determine the official SJT band.

If exact fidelity is required, request written clarification from the UCAT
Consortium or Pearson before changing the permutation matrix. The public sources
reviewed here support partial credit and an eight-point maximum, but not a more
precise claim.
